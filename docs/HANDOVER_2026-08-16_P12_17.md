## 1. The bug

```python
def _week_start_utc(now=None):
    now = now or datetime.now(timezone.utc)
    start = now.replace(hour=0, minute=0, second=0, microsecond=0)
    start = start.replace(day=start.day - start.weekday())  # Monday
    return int(start.timestamp() * 1000)
```

`datetime.replace(day=...)` sets the day **within the current month**; it
cannot cross a boundary. When the current week began in the previous month,
`start.day - start.weekday()` is zero or negative:

| Date | Weekday | `day - weekday()` | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-01 | Tue (1) | `1 - 1 = 0` | `ValueError` |
| 2026-11-01 | Sun (6) | `1 - 6 = -5` | `ValueError` |
| 2026-01-01 | Thu (3) | `1 - 3 = -2` | `ValueError` (week began in 2025) |

A sweep of 2026 found **38 failing days** — about one day in ten, every week
that spans a month end.

## 2. Why it mattered

The weekly loss limit is one of the two hard limits in decision B6, so
`_week_start_utc()` is on the hot path of every gate evaluation. On a failing
date the gate did not return "open" or "closed" — it **raised**. What happened
next depended entirely on whether the caller sat inside a `try`:

| Caller | Behaviour on a failing date (measured) |
|---|---|
| `daily_weekly()` / `evaluate()` / `status_text()` | `ValueError` |
| `main.py approve` | propagates the `ValueError` at the human |
| Paper runner enrolment | fails closed, but records `"day is out of range for month"` as the reason |
| `brain.immune.check_risk_system()` | **no `except`** — raises, taking `run_health_check()` down with it |

The last row is the worst one. The immune system's entire purpose is to notice
a broken risk system, and on these dates it was the component that broke
hardest — reporting nothing at all rather than a critical alert.

A gate that raises is neither open nor closed. That is the most dangerous
state a risk control can occupy, because the "decision" becomes an accident of
exception handling.

## 3. The fix

```python
return int((start - timedelta(days=start.weekday())).timestamp() * 1000)
```

`timedelta` arithmetic crosses months and years correctly. Verified by an
exhaustive sweep of every date from 2024 through 2029 at three hours each
(6,576 datetimes): always exactly the correct Monday 00:00 UTC, never a crash,
`weekday() == 0` in every case.

**Defence in depth.** `check_risk_system()` now wraps the gate call and reports
`CRITICAL` with the underlying error when the gate cannot be evaluated:

```python
except Exception as exc:
    return {"name": "risk_system", "status": "CRITICAL",
            "detail": f"Risk gate could not be evaluated: {type(exc).__name__}: {exc}"}
```

The date arithmetic is fixed, but the *class* of failure — a risk gate that
raises — must never again be silent. `run_health_check()` keeps reporting its
other checks alongside.

## 4. A note on the probe's expectations

Cases C, D and E initially read "not fixed" after the fix landed, and the
reason is worth recording: my first expectations were wrong, not the code.

- **C / E:** on an empty book the standing B10 rule ("setup unproven at the
  student level") legitimately blocks enrolment and approval on *every* date.
  I had expected `enrolled=1`. The correct contract is not "the trade
  proceeds" but "the decision is made for a **risk** reason, with no date
  error" — the probe now asserts exactly that, and prints the post-fix reason
  so a reviewer can see it is `setup unproven`, not a crash.
- **D:** overall health is `CRITICAL` in a fresh checkout because the bundled
  sample candles are stale — unrelated to this bug. The case now asserts the
  `risk_system` verdict specifically rather than the aggregate.

Tightening the assertions to the real contract, rather than loosening them
until they passed, is what separates a regression harness from a rubber stamp.
