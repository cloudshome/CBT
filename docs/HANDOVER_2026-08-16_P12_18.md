## 1. Why this was next

P12.8's own handover states the doctrine plainly:

> "A measurement only matters where something acts on it."

It then listed the two sites it found acting on gross numbers —
`multiplier_from_score()` and `setup_proven()` — and fixed them. The audit
was correct but incomplete: it enumerated the sites that act on *expectancy*
and missed the sites that act on *P&L*. Those are the hard limits in decision
B6, and they act harder than anything else in the system, because they are the
desk's stop-loss on itself.

| Surface | Anchor it read |
|---|---|
| `closed_pnl()` → daily / weekly loss limits | `rr_achieved` (gross) |
| `drawdown()` → reduce_risk / stop_and_review / full_review ladder | `rr_achieved` (gross) |
| `brain/metrics.py` → equity, expectancy, PF, win rate, streaks | `rr_achieved` (gross) |

Meanwhile the P12.6 strategy scorecard, the quality score, the strategy score
and the Monte Carlo table were all net. The project was still measuring with
one number and deciding with another — just one layer down from where P12.8
looked.

## 2. Why it bites hardest on the live book

`close_paper_trade()` writes `rr_achieved` and nothing else:

```sql
UPDATE paper_trades SET status='CLOSED', outcome=?, exit_price=?, rr_achieved=?, ...
```

There is no `rr_net` column in that statement. **Every live paper row has
`rr_net IS NULL`.** Backtest and simulator rows carry a stored net (P12.6), so
any test or surface built on those looks fine — the gap is invisible unless you
look specifically at the live book.

Costs for live rows existed only as a compute-on-read step
(`brain.analytics._net_outcome()`), which the Monte Carlo surfaces already
used and the limits never did. Case E of the probe pins this so a future
reader knows it is the normal path, not an edge case.

## 3. What was measured (probe cases A–F)

All figures from the repo's own cost model, nothing hypothesised:

| Case | Gross reading | Net reality |
|---|---|---|
| **A** daily limit, 8 × −0.4R | −0.80% → gate **open** | −1.09% → past the 1.0% limit |
| **B** weekly limit, 16 × −0.4R | −1.60% → gate **open** | −2.19% → past the 2.0% limit |
| **C** drawdown, 32 × −0.5R | 4.00% → `normal` | 5.18% → `reduce_risk` |
| **D** scorecard, 6 × +1.5R / 8 × −1.0R | **+0.071R — an edge** | **−0.076R — a losing book** |
| **E** live row provenance | `rr_net IS NULL`, net ≠ gross | (the normal path) |
| **F** control, `rr_net == rr_achieved` | unchanged | unchanged |

A and B are **fail-open**: the desk is past its loss limit once fees are paid,
and the gate lets it keep trading. D is the one to show a reviewer — the
business scorecard reports a positive edge on a book that loses money.

## 4. The fix

```python
data.costs.net_r_of_row(row)   # the single definition
    stored rr_net wins                       (backtest / simulator rows)
    else compute from entry/stop/action/hold (the live path)
    else 0.0                                 (no gross R = contributes nothing)
```

Placed next to the cost model, not in `analytics`, because `brain/risk_gate.py`
and `brain/metrics.py` both needed it and `analytics` already imports from
neither — putting it in `data/costs.py` keeps the dependency direction clean
and gives the three surfaces one definition to share (invariant #10).

`closed_pnl()`, `drawdown()`, `_equity_curve()` and `_metrics()` all read it.
`_net_outcome()` now delegates to it. A **win is now a net win**: a trade that
cleared its target but not its fees is no longer counted as a winner, which
was previously flattering both the win rate and the profit factor.

`closed_pnl()` also returns `gross_r_sum`, `gross_pct` and `cost_r`, and the
CLI prints them beside the net figure:

```
today    8 trades  -1.09%   (limit 1.0%)   [gross -0.80%, costs 1.18R]
GATE: CLOSED — no new trades
  ✗ daily loss limit reached (-1.09% ≤ -1.0%)
```

**No threshold moved.** Only the number compared against them changed.

## 5. On the four pre-existing tests I touched

Four tests failed after the fix, and it matters *why* each one failed:

- `test_livebook_isolation.py` (×3) and `test_week_boundary.py` (×1) assert
  exact R figures (`-2.25%`, `6.0%` drawdown) while testing **source
  isolation** and **date arithmetic**. Their fixtures now set
  `rr_net == rr_achieved`, making the rows explicitly cost-free.
- `test_journal_metrics.py` (×2) pins the B4 arithmetic itself
  (PF = 12/4 = 3). Same treatment.

The alternative — relaxing them to `approx` or recomputing the expected values
against the cost model — would have made a future change in fee assumptions
silently rewrite what "source isolation" or "profit factor" means. Pinning
them cost-free keeps each test testing the one thing it was written for, and
cost behaviour is covered separately and explicitly in `test_net_limits.py`.

Two of those files are ones I wrote earlier this session, so this is my own
contract being tightened, not someone else's being bent.
